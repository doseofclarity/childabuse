Child Abuse

"All forms of physical and emotional ill‑treatment, sexual abuse, neglect, and exploitation that results in actual or potential harm to the child's health, survival, development, or dignity." – WHO / Philippine RA 7610

Child Abuse Like You Are 5 Years Old

🧸 Every child deserves to feel safe, loved, and protected – like a little puppy in a warm home.

• Physical abuse – when someone hurts a child's body (hitting, shaking, burning).
• Emotional abuse – when someone says mean things that make a child feel sad or scared all the time.
• Sexual abuse – when someone touches a child in private parts in a wrong way.
• Neglect – when grown‑ups forget to give food, clothes, or take a child to the doctor.
• Munchausen by proxy – when a caregiver makes a child sick on purpose to get attention.

🩺 Nurses and doctors are like superheroes – they must tell someone (like social workers or police) so the child can be safe.

Definition & Epidemiology

Child abuse – any act or failure to act that results in serious physical or emotional harm, sexual abuse, or exploitation of a child under 18 years. Philippine law (RA 7610) defines child abuse as "the maltreatment of a child, whether habitual or not, including psychological and physical abuse, neglect, and sexual abuse."

📊 Global prevalence (WHO)
• Physical abuse: 22.6% of children worldwide
• Emotional abuse: 36.3%
• Neglect: 16.3%
• Sexual abuse: 12.7% (girls), 7.6% (boys)
🇵🇭 Philippines (UNICEF, DOH)
• 1 in 2 children experience some form of violence (2015 National Baseline Study).
• Most common: physical discipline (59%), psychological aggression (50%), neglect (17%).
• Online sexual exploitation of children (OSEC) is a growing crisis – Philippines is a global epicenter.
⚠️ Key point: Child abuse often occurs in the family environment, and perpetrators are usually known to the child. Mandatory reporting by nurses is required by Philippine law (RA 7610, RA 9262).

Types of Child Abuse

✋ Physical abuse
Non‑accidental physical injury (hitting, shaking, burning, biting, fractures). Shaken baby syndrome is a severe form.
😢 Emotional abuse
Rejection, isolation, terrorizing, ignoring, verbal assault. Often accompanies other forms. Long‑term psychological damage.
🔞 Sexual abuse
Any sexual act with a child (molestation, rape, exploitation, pornography, online sexual abuse). Includes non‑contact acts (exhibitionism, grooming).
🏠 Neglect
Failure to provide basic needs: food, shelter, clothing, medical care, education, supervision. Most common but under‑reported.
🏥 Munchausen by proxy (factitious disorder imposed on another)
Caregiver (usually mother) fabricates or induces illness in a child to gain medical attention. Diagnostic clue: symptoms disappear when child separated from caregiver.
📌 Pillitteri note: Any injury that is inconsistent with the history given or with the child's developmental stage should raise suspicion of abuse.

Clinical Features & Red Flags

🔴 Physical abuse red flags
• Bruises in various stages of healing, pattern injuries (belt, hand, cigarette burns)
• Fractures in non‑ambulatory infant (ribs, metaphyseal corner fractures)
• Retinal hemorrhages, subdural hematomas (shaken baby)
• Delay in seeking care, inconsistent or changing history
🔴 Behavioral signs (all types)
• Fear of going home or of parents
• Aggression, withdrawal, developmental regression
• Sexualized behaviour, knowledge beyond age
• Self‑harm, suicidal ideation (older children)
• Poor school performance, chronic absenteeism
🔍 Suspicious injury patterns: Posterior trunk, buttocks, cheeks, ears, frenulum tears, genital area. Any injury in an infant not yet crawling or walking should be investigated.

Diagnosis & Mandatory Reporting

  • History taking: Interview child alone (age‑appropriate), use open‑ended questions, avoid leading questions. Document exact words.
  • Physical examination: Head‑to‑toe with careful documentation of injuries (body map, photography).
  • Diagnostic tests: Skeletal survey for suspected physical abuse in children under 2 years. CT/MRI for head trauma. Coagulation studies to rule out bleeding disorders.
  • Reporting (Philippine law): RA 7610 (Special Protection of Children Against Abuse) and RA 9262 (Anti‑Violence Against Women and Their Children Act) mandate that any person who has knowledge of child abuse must report to the Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD) or law enforcement. Failure to report may result in legal liability.
⚖️ Legal duty of nurses: Nurses are mandatory reporters. Report within 48 hours to the nearest DSWD office or barangay. The report must include the child's identity, circumstances, and any supporting evidence. Good faith reporting is protected.

Management & Nursing Care

👩‍⚕️ Immediate medical care
• Treat acute injuries (fractures, head trauma, burns)
• STI prophylaxis and pregnancy testing for sexual abuse
• Forensic evidence collection (rape kit) with proper chain of custody
• Provide emotional support and safety planning
🧠 Long‑term nursing interventions
• Coordinate with multidisciplinary team (social worker, psychologist, legal advocate)
• Provide trauma‑informed care – build trust, avoid re‑traumatisation
• Educate child about body safety, "good touch – bad touch"
• Support foster placement or safe family reunification
• Document everything objectively, use direct quotes
📌 Pillitteri priority: "The nurse's first responsibility is to ensure the child's immediate safety. Remove the child from the abusive environment if necessary, following legal protocols."

Prevention & Legal Aspects (RA 7610, RA 9262)

🛡️ Primary prevention strategies
• Parent education (positive discipline, child development, stress management)
• Home visitation programmes (nurse‑family partnership)
• School‑based body safety programmes
• Community awareness campaigns against child abuse
• Strengthening family economic supports
⚖️ Philippine laws protecting children
• RA 7610 (1992): Special Protection of Children Against Abuse, Exploitation and Discrimination Act – penalises physical, sexual, and psychological abuse.
• RA 9262 (2004): Anti‑Violence Against Women and Their Children Act – includes protection orders, criminalises psychological violence.
• RA 9775: Anti‑Child Pornography Act.
• RA 11930: Anti‑Online Sexual Abuse or Exploitation of Children (OSAEC) Act.
📞 Reporting hotline (Philippines): DSWD National Child Protection Hotline: 0939-112-1662 / 0931-016-1931. Aling Bata (Child Helpline) 163. Nurse's role: document, report, do not investigate beyond gathering necessary medical information.
